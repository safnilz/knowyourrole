FOOD IS NOT WASTE® Simulation

Select the role assigned to you. During the simulation, think and make decisions as this actor.

SIMULATION ROLES

Local Producer

WHAT YOU DO

You grow or produce food locally and supply it to manufacturers, distributors, retailers, hospitality businesses or other buyers.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Matching production with demand
  • Product quality and freshness
  • Harvest and production timing
  • Customer commitments
  • Storage capacity
  • Avoiding unsold surplus
  • Protecting your margins

WHAT YOU CAN DO

  • Adjust future production
  • Change harvest or production timing where possible
  • Communicate early with buyers
  • Find alternative customers
  • Redirect suitable surplus
  • Improve forecasting using demand information
DURING THE SIMULATION, ASK YOURSELF
  • “How much should I produce?”
  • “What demand information do I actually have?”
  • “What happens if my buyer suddenly needs less?”
  • “Can I redirect surplus before its value is lost?”
  • “Am I preventing waste or simply passing the problem downstream?”

Importer / Manufacturer

WHAT YOU DO

You import food or ingredients into the market, or turn ingredients into finished food products for other businesses and consumers.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Demand forecasts
  • Import and production lead times
  • Inventory levels
  • Shelf life
  • Production efficiency
  • Customer orders
  • Storage capacity
  • Cost and margins

WHAT YOU CAN DO

  • Adjust future orders or production
  • Change production volumes
  • Delay or modify orders where possible
  • Find alternative customers or channels
  • Redirect suitable surplus
  • Share inventory and demand information earlier
DURING THE SIMULATION, ASK YOURSELF
  • “Am I bringing or producing more than the system needs?”
  • “What information would have helped me forecast better?”
  • “Can existing stock be redirected?”
  • “What happens downstream if I continue with the original plan?”
  • “Am I solving the problem or transferring it to someone else?”

Distributor / Cold Chain

WHAT YOU DO

You store and transport food between different actors while maintaining the conditions needed to protect its quality and safety.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Inventory
  • Delivery schedules
  • Cold-chain integrity
  • Storage capacity
  • Transport capacity
  • Product shelf life
  • Customer commitments
  • Cost of holding and moving stock

WHAT YOU CAN DO

  • Hold stock where safe and practical
  • Change delivery timing
  • Reroute products
  • Consolidate or modify deliveries
  • Coordinate between suppliers and customers
  • Identify alternative destinations before products lose value
DURING THE SIMULATION, ASK YOURSELF
  • “Where is the food right now?”
  • “How much shelf life remains?”
  • “Can I safely hold it?”
  • “Can I reroute it somewhere useful?”
  • “Who needs to know immediately?”
  • “Will my decision prevent waste or just move the problem elsewhere?”

Retailer

WHAT YOU DO

You sell food directly to consumers and manage stock across stores or other retail channels.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Customer demand
  • Availability
  • Stock levels
  • Shelf life
  • Product appearance and quality
  • Storage and shelf space
  • Sales and margins
  • Unsold inventory

WHAT YOU CAN DO

  • Adjust future orders
  • Change merchandising or promotions
  • Mark down suitable products
  • Transfer stock between locations
  • Redirect eligible surplus
  • Share better demand information with suppliers
DURING THE SIMULATION, ASK YOURSELF
  • “What is actually likely to sell?”
  • “How much shelf life remains?”
  • “Can I change price, promotion or location?”
  • “Can suitable surplus be redistributed?”
  • “Should I reduce future orders?”
  • “Does my decision create pressure somewhere else?”

Hospitality / F&B

WHAT YOU DO

You purchase, prepare and serve food through hotels, restaurants, catering, events or other food-service operations.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Guest numbers
  • Customer experience
  • Food safety
  • Menu availability
  • Purchasing and preparation
  • Labour and operating costs
  • Portioning
  • Surplus food

WHAT YOU CAN DO

  • Adjust purchasing
  • Change preparation quantities
  • Adapt menus where practical
  • Improve portion planning
  • Use suitable ingredients elsewhere
  • Redirect safe edible surplus
  • Communicate demand changes earlier to suppliers
DURING THE SIMULATION, ASK YOURSELF
  • “How many people am I actually serving?”
  • “What has already been ordered?”
  • “What has already been prepared?”
  • “What can still be changed?”
  • “Can safe surplus be redirected?”
  • “What could I have done earlier to prevent this situation?”

Corporate / Institutional Buyer

WHAT YOU DO

You purchase food, catering or food-service requirements for workplaces, schools, hospitals, events or other organisations.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Expected headcount
  • Service requirements
  • Budgets
  • Quality
  • Reliability
  • Employee/guest experience
  • Contract commitments
  • Avoiding unnecessary orders

WHAT YOU CAN DO

  • Improve headcount forecasting
  • Adjust orders before cut-off times
  • Communicate changes earlier
  • Change service quantities
  • Coordinate with caterers
  • Plan for suitable surplus
  • Use previous consumption data to improve future orders
DURING THE SIMULATION, ASK YOURSELF
  • “How accurate is my expected headcount?”
  • “When is the last point at which I can change the order?”
  • “Who needs to know if demand changes?”
  • “Am I ordering a buffer simply to avoid running short?”
  • “What happens to food I order but do not use?”

Redistribution Partner

WHAT YOU DO

You help safe, edible surplus food reach people who can use it instead of allowing it to become waste.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Food safety
  • Remaining shelf life
  • Quantity
  • Collection timing
  • Transport
  • Storage
  • Recipient needs
  • Ability to distribute food quickly

WHAT YOU CAN DO

  • Assess whether food is suitable for redistribution
  • Match surplus with recipients
  • Arrange collection
  • Coordinate timing
  • Provide guidance on acceptable products
  • Redirect food to appropriate organisations
DURING THE SIMULATION, ASK YOURSELF
  • “Is the food still safe and suitable?”
  • “How much time do we have?”
  • “Who can receive it?”
  • “Can we collect and distribute it in time?”
  • “Do recipients actually need this product and quantity?”
  • “Could earlier communication have created a better outcome?”

Circular Resource Partner

WHAT YOU DO

You find productive uses for food or materials that can no longer remain in their original use, helping retain as much value as possible.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Material type
  • Condition
  • Contamination
  • Quantity
  • Consistency
  • Collection and transport
  • Processing capability
  • Value of the recovered output

WHAT YOU CAN DO

  • Assess available material
  • Identify suitable recovery routes
  • Separate materials where required
  • Convert suitable material into another useful resource
  • Coordinate collection and processing
  • Advise businesses on segregation requirements
DURING THE SIMULATION, ASK YOURSELF
  • “Can this still be used for its original purpose first?”
  • “If not, what is the highest-value next use?”
  • “Is the material properly separated?”
  • “Is there enough volume to recover it practically?”
  • “What contamination or logistical constraints exist?”
  • “Are we recovering value or unnecessarily choosing a lower-value option?”
SYSTEM PRINCIPLE

YOUR DECISION LENS

Whatever role you are playing, keep asking:

QUESTION 01
“What are you protecting?”
QUESTION 02
“What might your decision shift elsewhere?”
QUESTION 03
“What information would change your choice?”
QUESTION 04
“Are you preventing waste or transferring it elsewhere?”
PRACTICE PRINCIPLE

DON’T TRY TO FIND THE PERFECT ANSWER.

Make the decision your actor would realistically make with the information, incentives and constraints available to you.
