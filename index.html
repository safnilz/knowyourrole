SIMULATION ROLES

Local Producer

YOUR ROLE

You grow crops, raise livestock, or produce food locally. You supply fresh food to wholesalers, processors, retailers, hospitality businesses, or direct buyers.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Customer demand
  • Harvest timing
  • Yield & volume
  • Quality & freshness
  • Shelf life
  • Production costs
  • Buyer commitments
SEE AN EXAMPLE +
SITUATION

You planned to harvest 2 tonnes of fresh tomatoes for a customer tomorrow. The customer suddenly tells you they only need 60% of the original order. The tomatoes are perishable.

“What would you do?”

QUESTIONS TO THINK ABOUT
  • “What are you trying to protect first?”
  • “What information do you need before making a decision?”
  • “Who else in the food system could be affected by your choice?”
  • “What happens if you do nothing today?”
  • “Are you preventing waste — or moving the problem somewhere else?”

Importer / Manufacturer

YOUR ROLE

You import food ingredients or finished goods into the market, or manufacture food products for distributors, retailers, and food services.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Demand forecasts
  • Import lead times
  • Inventory levels
  • Production efficiency
  • Shelf life
  • Customer contracts
  • Cost and margins
SEE AN EXAMPLE +
SITUATION

A large shipment of imported dairy products arrives at the port with a shorter remaining shelf life than agreed with your retail buyer. The buyer refuses to accept the delivery under standard terms.

“What would you do?”

QUESTIONS TO THINK ABOUT
  • “What is your primary commercial risk right now?”
  • “What constraints are influencing your decision?”
  • “Who would you need to speak to across the supply chain?”
  • “What happens downstream if you continue with the original plan?”
  • “Are you solving the problem or transferring it to someone else?”

Distributor / Cold Chain

YOUR ROLE

You store and transport food between different actors while maintaining temperature-controlled conditions to protect quality and safety.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Delivery schedules
  • Cold chain integrity
  • Warehouse capacity
  • Transport costs
  • Product shelf life
  • On-time commitments
  • Holding costs
SEE AN EXAMPLE +
SITUATION

A retail customer delays an incoming delivery of chilled meats by 48 hours due to unexpected warehouse congestion. Your refrigerated trucks are queued outside and scheduled for other deliveries tomorrow.

“What would you do?”

QUESTIONS TO THINK ABOUT
  • “Where is the food physically right now, and how much shelf life remains?”
  • “What cost or constraint is compounding each hour you hold it?”
  • “Who needs to know about this bottleneck immediately?”
  • “What might another actor in the system want or need from you?”
  • “Will your decision prevent waste or just move the problem elsewhere?”

Retailer

YOUR ROLE

You sell food directly to consumers through supermarkets or grocery stores, balancing product availability on shelves with customer purchasing patterns.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Customer demand
  • Daily sales
  • Stock levels
  • Shelf life
  • Food safety
  • Storage & shelf space
  • Margin
SEE AN EXAMPLE +
SITUATION

Your supermarket has 500 packs of fresh yoghurt. Weekend demand was lower than expected, and 200 packs may expire within three days.

“What would you do?”

QUESTIONS TO THINK ABOUT
  • “What are you trying to protect first: revenue, shelf space, or brand reputation?”
  • “What information would help you make this decision faster?”
  • “Who else in the food system could be affected by your choice?”
  • “What happens if you wait another 24 hours?”
  • “Does your decision solve the issue or create pressure somewhere else?”

Hospitality / F&B

YOUR ROLE

You purchase ingredients, prepare meals, and serve food across hotels, restaurants, catering, or event operations under tight service deadlines.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Guest numbers
  • Customer experience
  • Food safety
  • Menu availability
  • Operating & food costs
  • Kitchen preparation timing
  • Storage capacity
SEE AN EXAMPLE +
SITUATION

A large corporate event booked for 300 guests has a last-minute turnout of only 180 people. A significant volume of prepared hot food and fresh buffet items remains unserved at the end of service.

“What would you do?”

QUESTIONS TO THINK ABOUT
  • “What has already been prepared versus what is still unopened ingredient?”
  • “What food safety or health regulations strictly constrain your choices?”
  • “Who would you need to speak to immediately to take action?”
  • “What information could have prevented this discrepancy earlier?”
  • “What might another actor want or need from you in this moment?”

Corporate / Institutional Buyer

YOUR ROLE

You procure food and catering services for workplaces, schools, hospitals, or events, managing annual budgets and attendee service satisfaction.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Expected headcount
  • Budget control
  • Service reliability
  • Employee/guest satisfaction
  • Contract commitments
  • Avoiding food shortages
  • Order deadlines
SEE AN EXAMPLE +
SITUATION

Your catering contract specifies a daily hot buffet for 250 staff. Due to hybrid working patterns on Thursdays, actual attendance drops to 140, leaving substantial unserved food every week.

“What would you do?”

QUESTIONS TO THINK ABOUT
  • “Why are you ordering the quantity you currently specify?”
  • “What penalty or complaint occurs if you run short versus if you over-order?”
  • “When is the cut-off point where you can modify orders with your caterer?”
  • “What constraints are currently shaping your contract terms?”
  • “What happens to the food that is paid for but unused?”

Redistribution Partner

YOUR ROLE

You operate non-profit food rescue organizations or charities, collecting safe, edible surplus food and distributing it to community recipients.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Food safety compliance
  • Remaining shelf life
  • Collection timing
  • Cold storage limits
  • Transport capacity
  • Recipient community needs
  • Distribution speed
SEE AN EXAMPLE +
SITUATION

A supplier calls offering 3 pallets of chilled sandwiches and fresh salads at 3:00 PM. The food expires in 24 hours. Your collection vans are already dispatched and your central chiller is 90% full.

“What would you do?”

QUESTIONS TO THINK ABOUT
  • “Is the food genuinely safe, suitable, and manageable in time?”
  • “What constraints are currently limiting your operational capacity?”
  • “Do recipients actually need this specific product and volume today?”
  • “What happens if you decline this offer?”
  • “Could earlier communication from donors have altered your capability?”

Circular Resource Partner

YOUR ROLE

You process inedible food, organic by-products, and agricultural residues into compost, animal feed, biogas, or secondary biomaterials.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU

  • Material contamination
  • Organic consistency
  • Volume & regular supply
  • Processing capacity
  • Logistics & haulage costs
  • Output product value
  • Operating licenses
SEE AN EXAMPLE +
SITUATION

A food producer offers you 5 tonnes of organic food waste daily, but it arrives mixed with plastic packaging, stickers, and cardboard liners. Your processing equipment will clog if contamination exceeds 2%.

“What would you do?”

QUESTIONS TO THINK ABOUT
  • “Could this material have been kept as edible food or higher-value resource first?”
  • “What is the true cost of sorting or rejecting contaminated material?”
  • “Who would you need to speak with to change how this is segregated?”
  • “What incentives does the waste generator have to cooperate with you?”
  • “Are you truly recovering value or simply acting as an alternative disposal route?”
